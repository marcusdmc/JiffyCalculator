How to Use the Calculator

  • Line up Film Speed with the Scene Number in the left window.
  • Equivalent exposures (Shutter Speed & f/Stop pairs) appear aligned in the right window.
  • In the left window, the EV can be found on the row matching Film Speed 100.
  • You can also align the EV with the Film Speed instead of Scene Number.
  • Reciprocity Failure: You must adjust exposure to compensate for reciprocity failure on long exposures (1s+). Check your film’s datasheet.

Moon Shots (Moon Only, Not Scenes)

Set Film Speed at Scene 1. For Full Moon, close lens down 1 stop; for Half Moon, use exposure on calculator; for Crescent Moon, open up lens 1 stop.
Note: Shutter speed must be 1/25s or faster or the moon will be blurred due to Earth’s rotation.

Star Trails

Line up Film Speed at Scene 26, and then convert shutter speeds to time exposures as follows: seconds become minutes and minutes become hours. Use long exposures!

General Instructions

  • For night photography, use fastest film possible. With black-and-white films, use tungsten indexes.
  • Flash-type and tungsten-type color films tend to give more normal renditions in “mazda” (incandescent) light. Daylight-type color film renders much warmer results, but is quite satisfactory.
  • It may be advisable at first, and especially with slower films, to bracket all exposures at least one stop over and one stop under.
  • NOTE: If exposures are slower than 1/25 second, USE TRIPOD.

How to Use The New Jiffy Calculator

1. Choose Your Film Speed (ISO): Click any ISO value in the left window (12 to 6400) or pick from the Film ISO dropdown to position the red hairline cursor.

2. Align Scene Number or EV: Drag the vertical slide strip up or down, move the Slide Position bar, or click any Scene in the right-hand directory so your Scene Number lines up directly across from your Film Speed.

3. Read Equivalent Exposures: Look across to the right window. Every f/Stop is now mechanically aligned with its exact Shutter Speed. Any combination in the right window yields the exact same exposure!

4. Check Tripod & Reciprocity Warnings: Exposures slower than 1/25s require a tripod, and exposures of 1 second or longer require film reciprocity compensation.
